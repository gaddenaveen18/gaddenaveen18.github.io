**What bridge would make tomorrow useful, trustworthy and within reach today?**

Imagine you've built something genuinely better. You show it to people. They love the idea. Then they buy the old product anyway. Why? Because the old one works today, it's affordable, and it fits the habits people already have. Your product may belong to tomorrow, but people have to decide today.

You could blame the market, or give up on the vision. Or you could ask what would make tomorrow easy to step into. This framework comes from the fourth book in my series on entrepreneurship in the superintelligence era. Carry one product, service or idea through it.

[![The Maker's Love, the Market's Choice](/assets/posts/the-makers-love-the-markets-choice.jpg)](/assets/posts/the-makers-love-the-markets-choice.jpg)

## Two sides of the river

- **The customer lives in today:** a need they can feel, what works now, what they can afford, the habits they already have, the curiosity to try and the freedom to say no.
- **The maker sees tomorrow:** a possibility nobody has asked for yet. The maker notices the gap, takes the risk, builds it well, sells honestly and keeps learning.

To me, the maker's love isn't *"love what I built."* It's *"I care enough about this to do it well for you."*

## Seven planks for the bridge

1. **Start with the person.** Good work starts with the person it serves, not the person who makes it.
2. **Notice the unsaid need.** A commuter doesn't ask for a new way to get around. They just wish the trip were easier. The need often exists long before anyone can put it into words.
3. **Serve today, build tomorrow.** Someone needs clean water now. Refusing to sell the bottle because a home purifier is coming someday helps nobody. The vision may belong to tomorrow; the business still has to live today.
4. **Make value easy to find and easy to trust.** Explain, show, price fairly, offer a trial, and take it back when it isn't the right fit. Invite people to experience it. Don't pressure them to accept it.
5. **Earn the trust.** Good intentions start trust. Honest actions earn it. Time proves it.
6. **Live, learn and grow.** Quality and ethics don't bend. The product, the price, the technology and even the original plan can. And sometimes letting go, on purpose, makes room for the next thing worth doing.
7. **Share the market.** Competition pushes quality up; collaboration widens the good. All should prosper, and I should be one among them.

I used to admire the giant leap. These days I'm less sure. The best path, in my experience, is a series of steps where the product, the people, the economics and the trust grow up together.

## Where profit fits

Profit is the salary for doing the business job well. It keeps the work alive. It isn't the reason people exist. And it's worth asking: profitable for when? Today, next quarter, or ten years from now?

## The key principle

**The maker doesn't own tomorrow. The customer doesn't have to imagine it alone. Good business builds the bridge between them.** That's where I've landed, for now. But this is a conversation, not a conclusion.

## Try it with your team

- What real need in someone's life gives our idea a reason to exist?
- What useful step could we offer today, without getting stuck there?
- Does our marketing make real value easier to discover, or just harder to ignore?
- Are we continuing because the need is still there, or because the product has become part of who we are?
