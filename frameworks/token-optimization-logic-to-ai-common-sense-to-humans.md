**"Look for the solution; it helps more than looking at the problem." 🤔**

Every AI request costs tokens, and tokens cost money (💰), time (⏳) and compute (🖥️). Most teams look at the problem: the bill is too high. This framework looks at the solution: who should be doing the optimizing?

## The question

What is the compound efficiency of good token use in the AI era?

- **A) Better ROI:** more value for every token spent.
- **B) Faster, familiar results:** speed and reliability users can count on.
- **C) A user-friendly interface:** easy to use, so people trust it.
- **D) All of the above. ✅**

The gains compound. Get token use right and all three improve together.

## AI-controlled vs user-controlled

Each line compares the same job, done two ways.

- **Context:** *AI-controlled*, RAG-powered retrieval pulls in focused, relevant context only. *User-controlled*, manual prompt engineering relies on trial and error.
- **Input:** *AI-controlled*, intelligent intake processing cleans and condenses each request. *User-controlled*, verbose, redundant input inflates token use.
- **Model:** *AI-controlled*, automated model selection picks the right-sized model for each task. *User-controlled*, people pick a model themselves, often the biggest one for every job.
- **Focus:** *AI-controlled*, dynamic context pruning keeps only what still matters. *User-controlled*, strategic focus drops as time goes to mechanics instead of outcomes.
- **Result:** *AI-controlled*, fewer tokens and more consistent results. *User-controlled*, higher token cost and inconsistent results.

## Two principles

- ✨ **Leave LOGIC to AI (token optimization).** Let the system retrieve, condense, route and prune. These are mechanical jobs that a well-built system does better and more consistently than people do by hand.
- ✨ **Keep COMMON SENSE with humans (context optimization).** People set the goal, decide what matters and judge the result. No system knows your purpose better than you do.

## The key principle

**AI-driven efficiency: automation handles the mechanics, and people stay accountable for the outcome.**

## Try it with your team

- Where are people hand-tuning prompts for work a system could handle?
- Do you send every request to the same model, whatever its size?
- Who decides what context matters, and is that decision written down?
