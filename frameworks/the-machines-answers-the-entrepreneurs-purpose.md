**If intelligence becomes abundant, what is still ours to do?**

For a long time, the hard part of making something was the making itself. Now AI can draft, design, code and test in an afternoon. So the question moves. If building gets easy, where does the real work go?

I don't have a neat answer. This framework comes from the first book in my series on entrepreneurship in the superintelligence era, and it works best if you carry one real need through it: something at work, at home or in your community that could work better. Don't solve it yet. Just bring it along.

[![The Machine's Answers, the Entrepreneur's Purpose](/assets/posts/the-machines-answers-the-entrepreneurs-purpose.jpg)](/assets/posts/the-machines-answers-the-entrepreneurs-purpose.jpg)

## What got abundant, and what didn't

- **Abundant:** drafting, designing, coding, testing, and an answer to almost any question.
- **Still scarce:** noticing the need, caring about it, deciding what matters, judging quality and keeping the trust.

Intelligence became abundant. Our attention didn't.

## Seven questions, one need

1. **When building gets easy.** For example, someone asks for help building a reminder app. Why? Because they keep missing their daughter's school events. The need was never the app. *Am I describing the real need, or the first solution that came to mind?*
2. **When purpose meets need.** Picture someone new on a job, stuck at four in the afternoon, and the right knowledge reaches them in plain words. *Did that help them do better, or replace them? Where would I draw the line?*
3. **The boss of tools.** Ten brilliant AI assistants sound wonderful, until managing them becomes a new job. Tools should work for us, and we should work for each other. *What would it cost me just to manage the help?*
4. **The product has a memory.** A restaurant changes its most popular dish: faster, cheaper, better on paper. Then the regulars stop ordering it. The numbers show *what* happened, not *why*. *Which assumption is my idea quietly depending on?*
5. **Protect the trust, shrink the promise.** You planned for ninety days and now you have thirty. Shrink the size of the promise, not the security, the core need or basic quality. *What is the smallest useful version, and what would I never compromise?*
6. **The seed and the soil.** Give two people the same AI and the same tools, and they still won't make the same thing. *What room would my idea need to grow?*
7. **Stay human, step sideways.** Before asking AI for another answer, look at the need again. *What if I'm looking at this from the wrong direction?* Sometimes the most thoughtful choice is not to build at all.

## Four reminders

- Possibility is not purpose.
- An answer is not a decision.
- Speed is not quality.
- A tool is not a need.

## The key principle

**When one's purpose meets another's need, magic happens.** When intelligence becomes abundant, someone still has to notice, and someone still has to care. That's where I've landed, for now. But this is a conversation, not a conclusion.

## Try it with your team

- Are we describing the real need, or the first solution someone suggested?
- If AI gave each of us two hours back every week, where would we put them?
- What is the smallest useful version we could deliver, and what would we never compromise to get there?
- Would we still care about this problem if AI had never suggested it?
