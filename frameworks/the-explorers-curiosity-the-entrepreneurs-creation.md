**Before curiosity becomes something, look again.**

Have you ever watched a young child discover something ordinary? It gets touched, turned, dropped, picked up and tried again. There's no goal yet, just curiosity. As we grow, we learn names, rules and patterns, and that helps us move faster. But once we know what something is, do we stop asking what else it could be?

This framework comes from the second book in my series on entrepreneurship in the superintelligence era. Carry one curiosity through it: something that keeps catching your attention. No need to turn it into a product yet.

## 1. Explore in a loop

**Notice → Wonder → Try → Learn → Retry → See again → Notice again.**

It isn't question, answer, finished. It's look, try, learn and look again. Sometimes the discovery happens not because the object changed, but because the person looking did. AI can research, compare and test hundreds of paths and speed the loop up. The question it leaves us is which curiosity deserves to continue.

The Explorer never travels empty-handed: skills, experience, mistakes, people, history and questions all come along. And meaningful work eventually reaches something one person doesn't know, so the better question may be *"What can I carry, and where do I need someone else?"*

## 2. Pass the gate between discovery and creation

Discovery gives us possibility. Creation asks for responsibility. Three questions I ask before creating:

- **Should it exist?** For me, ethics comes before excitement.
- **Who truly needs it, and when?** A real need, at the right time, ahead of other worthwhile needs.
- **What survives if Plan A fails?** I like having a Plan B and a simple fallback, so the need never depends on one perfect plan.

Then choose honestly: **yes, no, not yet, or explore again.** Going back to the loop isn't failure. Not every discovery deserves to become a product, and "not yet" can be as creative as yes. Would you add a fourth question?

## 3. Let the path evolve

Moving from A to B might mean walking, cycling, driving, flying or a rocket. The destination can stay the same while the path changes. A rocket is extraordinary, but walking is better when B is across the room.

- **Repeat** when the known path still fits.
- **Enhance** when the need has evolved.
- **Rethink** when the understanding was wrong.
- **Explore** when the territory is new.

Keep the purpose stable and the path flexible. For me, ethics, trust, security, quality and the core need are the boundaries. Almost everything else can change.

## 4. Let creation create again

A creation can succeed when people use it. It can also succeed when people create *because* of it: new knowledge, a stronger team, a new market, a new creator, or a question that didn't exist before. The product doesn't have to reproduce. The thinking can.

## The key principle

**Don't reset the Explorer. Refresh the Explorer.** Keep the history, the people, the lessons and the skill, then ask whether yesterday's interpretation still fits today. That's where I've landed, for now. But this is a conversation, not a conclusion.

## Try it with your team

- What have we stopped noticing because it has become too familiar?
- Is the problem we're solving repeated, evolved, or genuinely new?
- If Plan A changes, what should still survive?
- What can someone else create now that they couldn't before, because of our work?
