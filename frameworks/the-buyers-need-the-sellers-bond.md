**What turns a sale into trust, and trust into a bond?**

We all buy. Most of us sell something too: a product, an idea, ourselves in an interview. For example, imagine a mechanic who looks at your car and says, "Your brakes are fine for another six months. Come back then." He just turned down money. You'll probably go back to him for years.

This framework comes from the fifth book in my series on entrepreneurship in the superintelligence era. It looks at trust from both sides of the counter. Carry one buying or selling relationship through it: something you bought and still think about, or something you've sold.

[![The Buyer's Need, the Seller's Bond](/assets/posts/the-buyers-need-the-sellers-bond.jpg)](/assets/posts/the-buyers-need-the-sellers-bond.jpg)

## Same moment, two ways to handle it

- **The buyer's doubts.** *Transaction:* obstacles to defeat. *Bond:* the most honest feedback a seller will ever get. Sometimes the buyer is simply right.
- **What to sell.** *Transaction:* as much as possible. *Bond:* not less, not more, just what fits. Picture walking in for a simple phone for your parents and walking out with the top model and a plan they'll never use.
- **Honesty.** *Transaction:* after someone asks. *Bond:* before they ask, including "you may not need this yet."
- **The sale.** *Transaction:* the finish line. *Bond:* the starting line, where the buyer learns what the product really is and the seller learns from them.
- **When it breaks.** *Transaction:* make them fight for a refund. *Bond:* "That's on us. A new one ships today."
- **Staying.** *Transaction:* hard to leave. *Bond:* they stay because they want to.
- **Who wins.** *Transaction:* one side. *Bond:* both sides, in the same circle.
- **After years of trust.** *Transaction:* a guaranteed sale. *Bond:* a fresh choice and another conversation.

## Seven turns of the circle

**Need → Fit → Truth → Use → Fix → Both win → Next conversation.**

1. **Think like the buyer.** Hold two minds at once: one that knows the product, and one that asks, "Why would I choose this? Where might it let me down?"
2. **Sell what fits.** Fit isn't about cheap or expensive. It's about what this person needs, right now. There's a gray area: sometimes people want more than they need, and that's their choice.
3. **Say it before they ask.** Trust doesn't require perfection. It requires truth. Changing expectations are natural. Unspoken ones are dangerous.
4. **Treat the sale as a beginning.** Yesterday's buyer quietly helps tomorrow's buyer, even though they'll never meet.
5. **Handle what goes wrong.** Trust isn't really tested when things go right. It's tested in how a problem is handled.
6. **Let both sides win.** Unification is when buyer and seller feel they belong to the same circle. Profit belongs in that circle too. It just isn't permission to take everything possible from the buyer.
7. **Earn the next conversation.** Even after years of trust, the buyer doesn't belong to the seller.

## Healthy dependence, not captivity

A good product can become part of how you do things, the way bread becomes part of you. But one click to start shouldn't mean a dozen calls to cancel. Dependency can be natural. Captivity shouldn't be. You may see some lock-in as fair; I'm not sure there's one right line.

AI will get very good at matching, predicting and recommending. What stays human, I think, is the conscious part: understanding the person, the context, and what happens after we act.

## The key principle

**Build for the need. Sell for the fit. Let the buyer choose. Fix what goes wrong. Let trust earn the next conversation.** Unification doesn't mean loyalty forever. It means both sides can feel like winners, and both stay free to choose again. That's where I've landed, for now. But this is a conversation, not a conclusion.

## Try it with your team

- If we were the buyer, what would worry us?
- What expectation should we say out loud before anyone decides?
- If something went wrong tomorrow, how would we want it handled, and is that how we handle it today?
- Do our customers stay because they want to, or because leaving is hard?
